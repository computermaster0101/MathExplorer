Waves add up

Where two waves arrive in step, crest on crest, they add into a bigger wave. Where one's crest meets the other's trough, they cancel and the water stays still. Switch to How loud and you see the still lines fan out between two sources: that's how noise-cancelling headphones work, and why some seats in a hall sound dull.

Light through two slits

Send a straight wave at a wall with two narrow gaps. Each gap starts a new ring of waves, and the two rings add up into bright and dark bands beyond. In 1801 Thomas Young did this with light and showed that light is a wave. Narrow one gap and the waves bend round its edges: diffraction.

The Doppler effect and the sound barrier

A moving source bunches up the waves in front of it and stretches them out behind: that's why a passing siren drops in pitch. Push the source faster than the waves themselves can travel and they pile up into a V, a shock wave: the boom of a jet breaking the sound barrier, or the wake of a boat.

Chladni's sand

In 1787 Ernst Chladni scattered sand on a metal plate and stroked its edge with a violin bow. The plate rang, and the sand jumped away from the moving parts and gathered on the lines that stay still: the nodal lines. Each note gives its own pattern; higher notes make busier ones. Instrument makers still use this to tune violin backs and guitar tops.

Why a guitar doesn't sound like a flute

A plucked string doesn't just swing in one big arc. It vibrates in halves, thirds, quarters… all at once, each a harmonic at 2, 3, 4… times the pitch. Where you pluck decides how much of each: pluck near the end and you get lots of high harmonics, a bright twang; pluck in the middle and the even ones vanish, a soft round tone. That mix is the instrument's voice.

Any drawing from circles

Joseph Fourier showed that any repeating motion is a sum of simple circular ones. Here each circle spins at a whole number of turns per lap, riding on the one before, and the pen at the end draws the shape. A few circles give a rough sketch; more circles add the corners. The same idea compresses music into MP3s and photos into JPEGs: keep the big circles, drop the tiny ones.
