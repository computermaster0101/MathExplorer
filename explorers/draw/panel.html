Drawing anything with circles

Joseph Fourier claimed in 1807 that any wave, however jagged, is a sum of smooth sine waves. Drawn in the plane, each sine wave is a circle spinning at a whole number of turns per lap, carried on the end of the one before. Add enough of them and they draw anything: a heart, a star, the letter π, your own scribble. Circles sets how many are used; Add the circles one by one shows the drawing getting closer as each one joins, and Keep each stage's drawing leaves the earlier attempts on, each in its own colour, so you can see every step at once. Your phone uses the same idea to compress music and pictures.

Gears and the spirograph

A wheel rolling inside a ring draws a curve that closes only when the wheel has turned a whole number of times. How many? Divide both teeth counts by the biggest number that goes into both (their greatest common divisor): the wheel's share is the number of laps, the ring's the number of petals. Teeth counts that share no factor give the most intricate patterns, which is why spirograph sets have odd-looking numbers.

Pictures of music

A harmonograph's pendulums, or the two tuning forks behind a Lissajous figure, swing at speeds in a ratio. Simple ratios are the harmonies the ear finds sweet, and draw simple figures: 2 : 1 is an octave, 3 : 2 a fifth, 4 : 3 a fourth. Complicated ratios make busier figures, as they make busier sounds. Pythagoras noticed the musical half of this with strings 2,500 years ago.

Times tables on a circle

Put 200 points round a circle and join each point to the one twice its number round: the threads outline a cardioid. Times 3 gives a nephroid, times 4 a curve with three cusps. In between, at times 2.5 or 3.7, the threads tangle and untangle as the multiplier slides. Mathologer's video of this has been watched by millions.

Roses

Let the distance from the middle swing in and out, cos(nθ/d), as the angle θ turns. Whole numbers give petals: n of them if n is odd, 2n if even. Fractions make the pen go round several times before the curve closes, laying petals over petals.
