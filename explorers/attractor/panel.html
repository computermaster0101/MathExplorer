Chaos: rules without randomness

Every system here follows an exact rule: nothing is random. Yet the path never repeats, and you can't predict it far ahead. That is chaos: tiny differences at the start grow, doubling again and again, until two paths that began side by side have nothing in common. The chaos rate in Numbers measures how fast.

Strange attractors

Wherever you start, the point is pulled onto the same shape and then wanders over it forever. That shape is an attractor. These ones are strange: they are fractals, with structure at every scale, so their dimension isn't a whole number. The Lorenz attractor is about 2.06-dimensional: a little more than a surface.

Stretch and fold

How can paths spread apart forever and still stay on a shape of fixed size? The flow stretches every small blob of starting points, then folds it back over itself, like kneading dough. Watch the Rössler band: it spreads out flat, lifts, folds over and falls back in. Do that again and again and you get chaos.

Maps: jumping instead of flowing

A map jumps the point straight to its next place instead of moving it smoothly. Jump millions of times and colour each spot by how often it was landed on, and the picture appears like a photograph developing. The Hénon map is the simplest chaotic one: zoom into its curve and each line becomes a bundle of finer lines.
